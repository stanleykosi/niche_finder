# YouTube Shorts Research, Production and Scaling Guide

## A reusable operating framework for building viral YouTube Shorts channels

---

## 1. Purpose of This Guide

This guide defines a repeatable system for:

* Finding promising YouTube Shorts niches.
* Identifying viral video formats.
* Generating related video ideas.
* Sourcing suitable clips.
* Writing short-form scripts.
* Producing AI voice-overs.
* Editing videos for retention.
* Adding captions, music and sound effects.
* Publishing consistently.
* Reading performance data.
* Doubling down on successful formats.
* Outsourcing production.
* Scaling into multiple channels.

The central operating model is:

> Find proven demand, identify why the best videos work, reproduce the structure with a new angle, measure the result, improve it and repeat.

The objective is not to create random Shorts and hope that one succeeds.

The objective is to build a repeatable system for discovering, producing and scaling formats that are already showing strong audience demand.

---

# PART I: THE CORE SHORTS OPERATING MODEL

## 2. The Three Main Capabilities

A successful Shorts operation requires three capabilities:

### 2.1 Finding a strong niche

The niche must contain enough demand, clips, ideas and current momentum to support frequent uploads.

### 2.2 Creating viral videos

The production process must combine:

* A strong topic.
* A visually engaging opening.
* A curiosity-driven script.
* Fast pacing.
* Appropriate clips.
* A clear payoff.
* Retention-focused editing.

### 2.3 Scaling the system

Once the format is proven, the process should be:

* Documented.
* Delegated.
* Quality-controlled.
* Repeated across additional channels.

These stages must happen in order.

Do not attempt to scale a channel before learning how to make successful videos personally.

---

## 3. The Required Sequence

Use the following sequence:

1. Research a niche.
2. Find successful competitors.
3. Identify recent outlier videos.
4. Compare successful and unsuccessful uploads.
5. Determine the winning content mechanism.
6. Confirm that the niche is not excessively saturated.
7. Create and prepare the channel.
8. Generate ideas based on proven formats.
9. Source suitable clips.
10. Write the script.
11. Generate the voice-over.
12. Edit the video.
13. Add captions, music and sound effects.
14. Publish.
15. Study the statistics.
16. Improve weak elements.
17. Double down on outliers.
18. Reach monetization.
19. Document the process.
20. Train an editor.
21. Stabilize the channel.
22. Start the next channel.

Do not skip directly from niche selection to outsourcing.

---

# PART II: NICHE RESEARCH

## 4. Characteristics of a Strong Shorts Niche

A strong niche should have four important characteristics.

### 4.1 High idea ceiling

The niche must support many distinct video ideas.

A high idea ceiling means the channel can produce frequent content without repeating the exact same topic too quickly.

Questions to ask:

* Can this niche support at least dozens of videos?
* Are new topics or trends appearing regularly?
* Can the same format be applied to different subjects?
* Can one successful subtopic lead to several related videos?

Avoid formats that depend on one temporary event or one extremely narrow topic.

---

### 4.2 High clip ceiling

The niche must provide enough footage to support repeated production.

Questions to ask:

* Are there multiple clips available for each topic?
* Can several examples be shown in one Short?
* Are new clips appearing regularly?
* Can visually engaging footage be found quickly?
* Can the final explanation or reveal be shown clearly?

A strong idea is not enough if there is insufficient footage to execute it.

---

### 4.3 Proven audience demand

The niche should already have evidence of strong viewership.

Look for:

* Multiple videos with millions of views.
* Recent high-performing uploads.
* Channels receiving substantial monthly views.
* Repeated outliers using similar formats.
* More than one channel succeeding in the space.

The purpose of niche research is not to find something nobody has attempted.

The purpose is to find a proven audience opportunity that can still be entered and improved.

---

### 4.4 Relatively low saturation

A niche can have strong demand and still be unsuitable if too many channels are already producing identical content.

A promising niche has:

* Several successful channels.
* Clear evidence of viewer interest.
* A manageable number of direct competitors.
* Existing videos that can still be improved.
* Enough variation to create a recognisable version of the format.

Avoid niches where hundreds of channels are producing the same topics, scripts, clips and editing quality.

---

## 5. Initial Discovery Process

Begin by browsing the YouTube Shorts feed.

When possible, browse while logged out so recommendations are less influenced by personal viewing history.

Open promising channels in separate tabs.

Do not commit to the first channel or format that appears interesting.

Collect several possibilities before comparing them.

While browsing, prefer content with:

* English voice-over.
* A repeatable structure.
* Relatively simple production.
* Strong recent views.
* Obvious opportunities for improvement.
* A format that can be reproduced consistently.

English voice-over is preferred because it can support monetization and attract English-speaking audiences.

---

## 6. Look for Outlier Videos

An outlier is a video that performs significantly better than the channel’s normal uploads.

For example, if most videos receive tens of thousands of views but several receive:

* 3 million views.
* 5 million views.
* 7 million views.

Those videos should be studied closely.

For each competitor, identify:

* The channel’s normal performance range.
* The highest-performing recent videos.
* Videos that are dramatically above the normal range.
* Similarities between those videos.
* Differences between those videos and weak uploads.

Do not assume that every video on a successful channel represents a strong format.

A channel may upload several content styles while only one produces repeated viral results.

The objective is to isolate that successful style.

---

## 7. Prioritize Recent Outliers

Recent performance is more useful than historical performance.

Prefer outliers uploaded:

* Within the last few days.
* Within the last few weeks.
* During the current trend cycle.

Avoid selecting a niche only because a similar video went viral a year ago.

The format may no longer be active.

Ask:

> Is this type of video receiving strong views now?

The existence of several recent outliers is stronger evidence than one old viral upload.

---

## 8. Compare Winning and Losing Videos

Study both strong and weak videos from the same channel.

The winning videos show what works.

The weak videos help identify what is missing.

Compare the following:

* Topic.
* Hook.
* Opening visual.
* Script structure.
* Type of question created.
* Number of clips.
* Pacing.
* Reveal.
* Video length.
* Editing style.
* Music.
* Caption style.
* Final payoff.

Example:

A channel may post general dance commentary, streamer stories and unusual trend demonstrations.

If only the unusual trend demonstrations receive millions of views, the channel’s real winning mechanism is not simply “dance content.”

It may be:

> Difficult-looking trends where multiple people attempt the challenge and the final person reveals the correct method.

This deeper mechanism is more important than the broad niche label.

---

## 9. Identify the Viral Mechanism

Do not stop at describing the subject.

Determine why the viewer continues watching.

Possible mechanisms include:

* “How did they do that?”
* “Which person completed it correctly?”
* “What happens in the final clip?”
* “What is the hidden method?”
* “Why does this look impossible?”
* “Who failed and who succeeded?”
* “What is the explanation behind the trend?”

In the demonstrated trend format, the strongest videos contained:

* A visually confusing challenge.
* Several attempts.
* A final person who performs it correctly.
* A promised explanation.
* A curiosity-driven ending.

The viral mechanism was curiosity around an unusual visual action.

Ordinary dancing was not enough.

The video needed something visually special that made viewers question how it was done.

---

## 10. Spend Enough Time Researching

Do not complete niche research in a few minutes simply because one promising channel has been found.

Spend approximately one or two hours investigating:

* Multiple channels.
* Several recent uploads.
* High-performing videos.
* Weak videos.
* Shared patterns.
* Recent trend momentum.
* Production difficulty.
* Available footage.
* Saturation.

The purpose is to avoid building an entire channel around a misleading signal.

---

## 11. Use Channel-Level Performance Data

Use tools such as the vidIQ browser extension to examine:

* Views generated in the previous 30 days.
* Number of recent uploads.
* Upload frequency.
* Channel momentum.
* Whether views are consistent or dependent on one video.

A desirable signal is a channel or niche producing substantial recent views while maintaining a consistent upload schedule.

The guide’s example target is approximately 50 million or more recent views for a strong niche opportunity.

This is not the only acceptable number, but it provides evidence that sufficient demand exists.

Compare channels by:

* Recent views.
* Upload quantity.
* Viral frequency.
* Consistency.
* Production quality.

Do not choose a competitor only because it has a large subscriber count.

Recent view performance is more important.

---

## 12. Perform a Saturation Check

After identifying a promising format:

1. Copy the title of a successful video.
2. Search the title on YouTube.
3. Filter the search to Shorts.
4. Search the name of the trend.
5. Search related keywords.
6. Search relevant hashtags when available.
7. Open channels producing similar content.
8. Compare their recent results.

### Positive saturation signal

Several channels dedicated to the format are performing well.

This confirms that the opportunity is not limited to one creator.

### Negative saturation signal

Many channels produce similar videos at similar quality, but most receive poor views.

This can indicate:

* Excessive saturation.
* Weak overall demand.
* A creator-specific advantage.
* A format that is harder to reproduce than it appears.

### Strong entry signal

The best entry opportunity is:

* More than one successful channel.
* Recent viral uploads.
* Clear viewer demand.
* A relatively small group of direct competitors.
* Noticeable room to improve the content.

# PART III: CHANNEL SETUP

## 13. Create or Select the Channel Account

Create a Gmail account and YouTube channel, or use an existing account.

An older account that has already been used to watch YouTube is preferred.

Before posting on a completely new account, warm it up for a few days.

Behave like a normal YouTube user:

* Watch videos.
* Browse different channels.
* Interact naturally with the platform.
* Build some viewing history.

The objective is to make the account appear established and legitimate rather than newly created for automated uploading.

---

## 14. Keep Branding Simple

Do not spend excessive time on:

* Profile pictures.
* Banners.
* Channel names.
* Colours.
* Fonts.
* Complex visual identity.

Initial branding should take approximately ten minutes.

Content performance is more important than channel artwork.

---

## 15. Profile Picture Creation

Use an AI image generator to create a simple image related to the niche.

The prompt should include:

* The channel topic.
* The intended visual style.
* The main subject.
* Desired colours.
* Whether text should be excluded.
* The intended use as a YouTube profile image.

If the first result is poor:

* Explain what is wrong.
* Ask the AI to revise it.
* Change the style.
* Simplify the prompt.
* Try another AI image generator.

The result only needs to look relevant and acceptable.

---

## 16. Banner Creation

Request a YouTube banner at approximately:

**2560 × 1440 pixels**

The banner should:

* Match the niche.
* Contain simple visuals.
* Avoid unnecessary text.
* Keep important elements within the centre-safe area.

If the AI does not follow the banner format:

* Supply a banner template.
* Ask the AI to fill the central safe area.
* Crop the generated image.
* Adjust it in Canva.
* Try another image generator.

Do not allow banner creation to delay the launch.

---

## 17. Channel Name Creation

Use AI to generate approximately 20 possible names.

The prompt should request names that are:

* Short.
* Easy to remember.
* Original.
* Relevant to the niche.
* Suitable as a handle.

Review the suggestions and:

* Select the strongest name.
* Combine words from different suggestions.
* Remove overly complicated names.
* Avoid names that are difficult to spell.

The name does not need to be perfect.

It needs to be usable.

---

# PART IV: VIDEO IDEA GENERATION

## 18. Begin With Proven Formats

Return to the competitor’s strongest videos.

Identify the exact format that repeatedly performs.

For example:

* How a difficult trend is performed.
* Several people trying an impossible challenge.
* A final participant revealing the correct method.
* A trend going wrong.
* A confusing visual with an explanation.

Do not ask AI for random ideas without context.

Use the proven structure as the starting point.

---

## 19. Prompt AI With Complete Context

When asking AI to generate ideas, provide:

* The fact that the content is for YouTube Shorts.
* The channel niche.
* A link or description of the successful competitor video.
* The type of trend being studied.
* Why the reference video is engaging.
* The need for recent ideas.
* The need for visually confusing or unusual actions.
* The requirement that the topic supports a reveal.

AI output improves when the prompt clearly explains what is required.

Do not assume the AI automatically understands:

* The target platform.
* The desired format.
* The reason the original video performed.
* The required level of curiosity.
* Which suggestions should be excluded.

---

## 20. Search for Current Trends Manually

Use TikTok and YouTube to search for:

* The trend name.
* “Dance trend.”
* “Viral trend.”
* “How to do [trend].”
* Relevant hashtags.
* Recently uploaded examples.

Prioritize videos that:

* Were uploaded recently.
* Are accumulating strong engagement.
* Contain an unusual visual.
* Look difficult to understand.
* Create a natural “how?” question.
* Can support multiple clips and a reveal.

Avoid ordinary trends that do not contain anything surprising.

The topic should make viewers question what they are seeing.

---

## 21. Evaluate Each Idea

Before committing to an idea, confirm that it has:

* A strong opening visual.
* At least several usable clips.
* A clear explanation or revealing clip.
* Enough movement.
* A curiosity gap.
* A simple script.
* A satisfying final moment.
* Similarity to the proven format without being identical.

Reject ideas that:

* Look visually ordinary.
* Have no clear reveal.
* Require too much explanation.
* Have insufficient footage.
* Are already heavily repeated.
* Cannot be understood quickly.

---

## 22. Add a New Spin

Use the competitor’s structure but apply a different:

* Trend.
* Topic.
* Clip sequence.
* Hook.
* Reveal.
* Editing treatment.
* Visual angle.
* Supporting example.

Do not simply reproduce the same video.

The principle is:

> Copy the successful structure and add your own spin.

The new Short should preserve the psychological reason the original worked while changing the subject or execution.

---

# PART V: COMPETITOR VIDEO ANALYSIS

## 23. Reverse-Engineer the Video Before Editing

For each reference video, document:

* Total duration.
* First spoken line.
* First visible frame.
* Number of clips.
* Clip duration.
* Caption style.
* Caption timing.
* Music.
* Beat-drop position.
* Arrows or circles.
* Sound effects.
* Zooms.
* Camera movement.
* Colour treatment.
* Reveal timing.
* Ending structure.

Do not begin editing until the structure is understood.

---

## 24. Analyze the Hook

The hook should be evaluated visually and verbally.

Ask:

* Would the first frame attract attention without sound?
* Is there immediate movement?
* Is something unusual happening?
* Is the subject clear?
* Does the narration immediately explain the situation?
* Is there a reason to continue watching?

The strongest visual should be placed first, even if it is not the first event chronologically.

---

## 25. Analyze the Pacing

Count:

* How many times the visual changes.
* How long each clip remains on screen.
* Whether one clip is cut into multiple sections.
* How frequently the viewer receives new information.
* Whether the reveal arrives quickly enough.

The video should remain compact.

Remove:

* Pauses.
* Repeated explanations.
* Slow introductions.
* Empty frames.
* Unnecessary transitions.
* Visuals that do not support the narration.

---

## 26. Analyze the Payoff

The final part of the Short must fulfil the promise made by the hook.

If the video promises:

* The correct method.
* A surprising final participant.
* A reveal.
* An unusual result.

The final clip must clearly show it.

The viewer should understand why they remained until the end.

---

# PART VI: CLIP SOURCING

## 27. Find the Revealing Clip First

Before collecting many clips, search for a video that clearly exposes or demonstrates the method.

Search phrases such as:

* “How to do [trend].”
* “[Trend] tutorial.”
* “[Trend] explained.”
* “[Trend] behind the scenes.”
* “[Trend] trick.”

Confirm that a strong final payoff exists before building the rest of the video.

This prevents wasting time on a topic that cannot be completed effectively.

---

## 28. Collect a Complete Clip Package

A strong clip package should include:

1. A highly visual hook clip.
2. Several people attempting the trend.
3. Different angles or participants.
4. A funny or failed attempt.
5. The person who reveals the method.
6. A clear ending clip.

Prefer footage with:

* Strong movement.
* Clear subjects.
* Good lighting.
* Minimal obstructive text.
* A vertical or crop-friendly composition.
* An obvious action.

Avoid clips containing large captions in the centre when those captions cannot be removed easily.

---

## 29. Download and Organize Clips

Download the selected videos and rename them according to purpose.

Example naming structure:

* `01_hook`
* `02_attempt_one`
* `03_attempt_two`
* `04_failure`
* `05_reveal_person`
* `06_method`
* `07_final_reaction`

Organizing clips by narrative function makes editing faster.

---

# PART VII: SCRIPTING

## 30. Use a Short Viral Script Structure

The demonstrated script format contains four parts.

### Part 1: Introduce the subject

Example structure:

> “This couple tried the [trend name]…”

### Part 2: Explain the unusual effect

Example structure:

> “…where your legs disappear while your partner holds you…”

### Part 3: Create escalation

Example structure:

> “While most people thought they completed the trend…”

### Part 4: Promise and deliver the reveal

Example structure:

> “…the last person showed exactly how to do it, and the method will shock you.”

The script should be short enough to support a compact video.

---

## 31. Script Rules

The script must:

* Begin immediately.
* Avoid greetings.
* Avoid channel introductions.
* Avoid unnecessary background information.
* Explain what is happening.
* Create curiosity.
* Delay the answer briefly.
* Lead into the final reveal.
* Match the available clips.

Every sentence must serve the video.

Remove any sentence that does not:

* Explain.
* Escalate.
* Create curiosity.
* Deliver the payoff.

---

## 32. Use Competitor Transcripts as Structural References

To study a reference script:

1. Copy the competitor’s YouTube URL.
2. Paste it into a YouTube transcript extraction website.
3. Copy the transcript.
4. Separate the script into structural parts.
5. Identify the hook, escalation and reveal.
6. Rewrite the script for the new topic.

Do not copy the topic unchanged.

Retain the successful structure and replace the subject with the new idea.

---

## 33. Script Length

The script must fit the video’s successful niche length.

For a 15–20 second Short:

* Use approximately two or three compact sentences.
* Keep each phrase visually supportable.
* Avoid complex explanations.
* Reach the reveal quickly.

The example format works at approximately 16 seconds.

Other niches may require longer videos, so use successful competitors as the primary reference.

---

# PART VIII: AI VOICE-OVER

## 34. Voice Selection

Use an AI voice platform such as ElevenLabs.

For broad English-speaking Shorts, search for a voice with:

* English language.
* American accent.
* Conversational style.
* Social-media delivery.
* High quality.
* Young voice.
* Male voice where suitable.
* Energetic expression.

The voice should not be:

* Too soft.
* Too monotone.
* Unclear.
* Overly dramatic.
* Slow.
* Robotic.

A strong American accent is preferred for attracting a US audience and supporting a higher RPM.

---

## 35. Generate Line by Line

Do not generate the complete script in one attempt.

Separate it into individual sentences or short phrases.

For each line:

1. Read the line aloud in the intended style.
2. Generate the AI version.
3. Compare the rhythm.
4. Check pronunciation.
5. Check expression.
6. Regenerate when necessary.
7. Download the strongest version.

Generating line by line prevents one weak sentence from forcing the regeneration of the entire script.

It also saves voice-generation credits.

---

## 36. Voice Quality Control

Reject a generated line when:

* A word is mispronounced.
* The voice is too soft.
* The pacing is too fast.
* The emphasis is incorrect.
* The sentence sounds unnatural.
* The final word drops in energy.
* The expression does not match the visual.

Regenerate until the line sounds correct.

When repeated generations remain poor, adjust:

* Stability.
* Similarity.
* Style exaggeration.

The AI voice should be directed and reviewed rather than accepted automatically.

---

# PART IX: EDITING WORKFLOW

## 37. Recommended Editing Software

The demonstrated workflow uses DaVinci Resolve.

Other suitable options include:

* CapCut.
* Premiere Pro.
* Mobile captioning applications.

DaVinci Resolve’s free version can perform the main editing process.

CapCut is beginner-friendly, although several features require payment.

---

## 38. Begin With the Voice-Over

Import the voice-over before arranging video clips.

The narration should form the structure of the timeline.

Complete the following:

1. Place all narration lines in order.
2. Remove long gaps.
3. Ripple-delete empty spaces.
4. Listen to the complete voice-over.
5. Regenerate any rushed or weak line.
6. Confirm the final pacing.
7. Lock the narration structure.

The video clips should then be matched to the narration.

---

## 39. Use Efficient Editing Shortcuts

Use keyboard shortcuts for:

* Splitting.
* Ripple deletion.
* Copying.
* Pasting.
* Moving clips.
* Duplicating effects.
* Adjusting keyframes.

Production speed matters because the format is intended for daily publishing.

Small time savings become important across many videos.

---

## 40. Hook Editing Convention

The first clip must be the most visually engaging available footage.

Select footage containing:

* Strong movement.
* Multiple people.
* An unusual body position.
* A fall.
* A surprising reaction.
* A visually confusing action.

The opening must work even when muted.

Do not begin with:

* A slow setup.
* A static frame.
* A wide shot with unclear subjects.
* A title card.
* An introduction.
* A weak example saved only because it occurred first.

The strongest clip should lead.

---

## 41. Match Visuals to Spoken Phrases

Divide the narration into visual beats.

Example:

* “This couple tried…” → Show the main couple.
* “The owl trend…” → Show the unusual pose.
* “Where your legs disappear…” → Show the disappearing-leg effect.
* “Most people thought…” → Show multiple attempts.
* “The last guy…” → Introduce the final participant.
* “Showed exactly how…” → Show the revealing movement.
* “The method will shock you…” → Land on the strongest final moment.

Each clip should support the exact phrase being spoken.

---

## 42. Maintain Constant Visual Movement

The video should remain visually active.

Use:

* Clip changes.
* Reframed crops.
* Slow zooms.
* Subject tracking.
* Different angles.
* Movement within the source footage.
* Short visual sections.

Avoid leaving one unchanged visual on screen for too long.

More movement generally makes the Short feel more immersive.

---

## 43. Reuse the Reveal Subject Strategically

The person who delivers the final reveal may be shown briefly before the ending.

This creates anticipation and connects the narration to the payoff.

Do not show the complete method too early.

Use the earlier appearance as a teaser.

Return to the person during the final reveal.

---

## 44. Video Length Convention

Use competitor performance to determine the appropriate length.

General recommendation:

* Approximately 20 seconds or longer for many Shorts.
* Approximately 15–16 seconds can work for very compact visual trend formats.

Do not lengthen a simple idea unnecessarily.

Do not shorten a video so aggressively that the viewer cannot understand the reveal.

---

# PART X: MUSIC

## 45. Study Competitor Music Choices

Check whether successful competitors repeatedly use:

* The same song.
* The same song version.
* A sped-up version.
* Similar music across multiple uploads.
* A consistent beat-drop position.

Repeated use can indicate that the music is part of the recognised format.

---

## 46. Identify the Song

Use Shazam to identify the competitor’s background music.

Search for the exact version used, including:

* Sped-up version.
* Slowed version.
* Remix.
* Edited version.

Download the appropriate version and place it on the editing timeline.

---

## 47. Music Mixing Convention

The music must not compete with the narration.

Use the following approach:

1. Lower the music beneath the voice-over.
2. Keep narration clearly understandable.
3. Add volume keyframes.
4. Raise the music near the final reveal.
5. Align the beat drop with the strongest visual moment.
6. End the video cleanly after the payoff.

In the example, the beat drop is aligned with the participant falling or completing the visual action.

Music should reinforce the structure of the video.

---

# PART XI: ZOOMS AND VISUAL EFFECTS

## 48. Gradual Zoom Convention

Add adjustment clips or keyframed zooms over source footage.

Use slow, gradual zoom-ins to:

* Add movement.
* Increase immersion.
* Prevent static footage.
* Make separate clips feel more cohesive.

The zoom should not be excessively aggressive.

Copy the adjustment treatment across clips when suitable.

For longer or differently framed clips, adjust the keyframes individually.

---

## 49. Arrow and Circle Convention

Use arrows and circles to direct the viewer’s attention.

Suitable uses include:

* Identifying the main subject.
* Showing which person matters.
* Highlighting an unusual movement.
* Introducing the final participant.
* Directing attention during the hook.

Effects should appear quickly and remain on screen briefly.

If an arrow looks awkward, use a circle instead.

The effect’s purpose is more important than copying the competitor’s exact design.

---

## 50. Overlay Animation

Overlays should contain small amounts of movement.

Possible animations include:

* Pop-in.
* Quick scale-up.
* Brief bounce.
* Subtle rotation.
* Subject tracking.
* Fast disappearance.

Avoid overlays that block the important action.

Track circles or arrows when the subject moves.

---

## 51. Camera-Shake Convention

Apply subtle camera shake to important moments such as:

* A reveal.
* A fall.
* An arrow appearing.
* A major movement.
* A beat drop.

The effect must remain subtle.

Excessive shaking reduces clarity.

---

# PART XII: COLOUR AND ENHANCEMENT

## 52. Basic Colour Treatment

Use an adjustment layer to improve:

* Saturation.
* Highlights.
* Shadows.
* Contrast.
* Sharpness.

The objective is to make the clips:

* Brighter.
* Clearer.
* More visually consistent.
* More suitable for phone viewing.

Do not overgrade the footage.

The content should still look natural.

---

## 53. Sharpness and Enhancement

Increase sharpness moderately when footage appears soft.

Optional enhancement tools such as Topaz AI may be used to create a sharper or higher-quality appearance.

This is not required for the format to work.

Prioritize:

* Clear subjects.
* Strong movement.
* Good pacing.
* A visible payoff.

These are more important than extreme image enhancement.

---

# PART XIII: SOUND EFFECTS

## 54. Sound-Effect Placement

Add sound effects beneath important visual actions.

Suitable moments include:

* Arrow appearance.
* Circle appearance.
* Caption pop.
* Quick zoom.
* Camera effect.
* Reveal.
* Final visual.
* Transition.

Suitable sound types include:

* Clicks.
* Pops.
* Camera shutters.
* Short impact sounds.

---

## 55. Sound-Effect Volume

Sound effects must not be too loud.

Loud effects can:

* Clash with narration.
* Hurt the viewer’s ears.
* Cause an immediate swipe.
* Make the edit feel unprofessional.

The audio hierarchy should be:

1. Voice-over.
2. Background music.
3. Sound effects.

The narration must remain the clearest element.

---

# PART XIV: CAPTIONS

## 56. Caption Generation

After the main edit is complete:

1. Export or import the video into a captioning application.
2. Generate automatic captions.
3. Review every caption.
4. Correct the wording.
5. Correct the timing.
6. Break long phrases into shorter sections.
7. Confirm that captions match the spoken rhythm.

Automatic timing should not be trusted without review.

---

## 57. Caption Timing Convention

Captions must appear when the words are spoken.

Manually adjust:

* Start time.
* End time.
* Phrase divisions.
* Word timing.
* Transitions between captions.

Do not allow a caption to remain after the corresponding words have ended.

Do not allow the next caption to appear too early.

---

## 58. Caption Length Convention

Keep captions short.

Break sentences into natural spoken sections.

Example:

* “This couple”
* “tried the owl trend”
* “where your legs disappear”
* “and your partner holds you”

Avoid displaying an entire sentence at once.

Short caption chunks are easier to process on a mobile screen.

---

## 59. Caption and Clip Alignment

Avoid captions overlapping awkwardly between unrelated clips.

When the visual changes, the caption structure should continue to feel organised.

Do not allow:

* One phrase to remain across too many clips.
* Two captions to overlap.
* Captions to cover the key action.
* Captions to appear too close to interface elements.

---

## 60. Caption Styling

Use a font that is:

* Bold.
* Easy to read.
* Suitable for the niche.
* Slightly italic or curved where appropriate.

Add:

* A clear stroke.
* A drop shadow.
* Strong contrast from the footage.

Important words may be highlighted when useful.

Highlighting is optional.

Readability and timing are more important.

---

## 61. Export Settings

Use high-quality export settings.

The demonstrated caption export uses:

* 60 frames per second.
* High bit rate.

Before final export, watch the complete video and confirm:

* Captions are accurate.
* Timing is correct.
* The audio is balanced.
* The reveal is visible.
* The video ends cleanly.

---

# PART XV: TITLES AND UPLOADS

## 62. Title Convention

Keep the title simple and directly related to the trend.

Examples:

* “How to Do the Owl Trend”
* “Owl Trend Goes Viral”

The title should clearly communicate the topic.

Avoid unnecessarily complicated titles.

---

## 63. Daily Publishing Convention

Publish Shorts daily.

Set aside approximately two hours per day for:

* Research.
* Clip sourcing.
* Scripting.
* Voice generation.
* Editing.
* Captions.
* Uploading.

Consistency is essential.

Many creators fail because they stop posting before they have produced enough videos to improve or discover a winning format.

---

# PART XVI: ZERO-VIEW RESPONSE PROCEDURE

## 64. Zero-View Handling

When a newly uploaded video receives zero views:

### First zero-view upload

Take a one-day break before uploading again.

### Second consecutive zero-view upload

Take a two-day break before uploading again.

### Third consecutive zero-view upload

Create or use another channel.

Warm the new channel and continue the process.

Older accounts with normal viewing history are preferred because they appear more established.

---

# PART XVII: PERFORMANCE PLATEAUS

## 65. The 10,000–30,000 View Range

Do not assume the channel is permanently trapped when videos repeatedly receive approximately 10,000–30,000 views.

Continue posting stronger videos.

The objective is to produce a video that passes approximately 40,000 views and receives broader distribution.

Treat the plateau as a signal to improve:

* Topic selection.
* Hook.
* Pacing.
* Retention.
* Editing.
* Reveal.

Do not treat it as a reason to stop.

---

# PART XVIII: ANALYTICS

## 66. Main Performance Metrics

The two main metrics are:

* Viewed versus swiped away.
* Average percentage viewed.

These metrics help determine whether the problem is the hook or the body of the video.

---

## 67. Viewed-Versus-Swiped Target

Aim for approximately 80% of viewers choosing to watch rather than swipe away.

A low result can indicate:

* Weak opening footage.
* Poor topic.
* Slow first second.
* Unclear subject.
* Weak first sentence.
* Insufficient curiosity.

When the viewed rate is weak, improve the hook before changing the entire video.

---

## 68. Average Percentage Viewed

Aim for more than 100% average percentage viewed where possible.

A result above 100% means viewers are:

* Rewatching.
* Allowing the video to loop.
* Replaying the reveal.
* Watching certain parts multiple times.

For a video of approximately 15 seconds, target around 130% average percentage viewed.

Shorter videos generally need a higher percentage.

---

## 69. Diagnose the Problem

### Low viewed rate and low retention

Possible causes:

* Weak topic.
* Weak opening.
* Poor visuals.
* Slow pacing.
* Unclear promise.

### Strong viewed rate but weak retention

Possible causes:

* The hook works but the body is slow.
* The clips are repetitive.
* The script contains unnecessary information.
* The reveal takes too long.
* The payoff is weak.
* The video does not fulfil the opening promise.

### Strong viewed rate and strong retention

Create more videos using the same:

* Topic type.
* Hook structure.
* Visual mechanism.
* Editing style.
* Pace.
* Reveal pattern.

---

# PART XIX: DOUBLING DOWN

## 70. Identify Your Own Outliers

After publishing several videos, compare performance.

An outlier is one of your videos that performs substantially better than the rest.

Document:

* Topic.
* Hook.
* First frame.
* Script.
* Length.
* Clips.
* Music.
* Editing rhythm.
* Final reveal.
* Viewed rate.
* Average percentage viewed.

---

## 71. Produce Variations of Winners

When a video performs significantly better:

* Create more videos around the same topic.
* Use similar hooks.
* Find related trends.
* Repeat the same curiosity mechanism.
* Preserve the editing structure.
* Change the clips and subject.
* Test adjacent subcategories.

Continue producing around the successful genre until its performance declines.

Then find the next strong variation.

---

## 72. Allow the Niche to Evolve

A channel may begin with one category and later discover that a related category performs better.

For example:

* Start with dance trends.
* Discover that strange visual trends perform better.
* Expand into famous internet challenges.
* Continue focusing on the strongest-performing style.

Let performance determine the direction.

Do not remain attached to the original niche label when the data shows a stronger sub-niche.

---

# PART XX: MONETIZATION TARGET

## 73. Monetization Milestone

The target described in this system is:

**10 million Shorts views within 90 days**

After reaching the threshold:

1. Apply for YouTube monetization.
2. Create or connect an AdSense account.
3. Complete the required review.
4. Begin earning Shorts revenue after approval.

Although 10 million views appears large, one highly viral Short can contribute a significant portion of the target.

Continue doubling down once a video begins breaking through.

---

# PART XXI: OUTSOURCING

## 74. Do Not Outsource Too Early

Do not hire editors before learning how to produce successful videos personally.

A beginner who has not made a viral video cannot properly:

* Judge an editor.
* Explain the format.
* Identify weak pacing.
* Approve the right clips.
* Diagnose bad performance.
* Write effective instructions.

Avoid hiring freelancers and expecting them to discover the winning strategy for you.

First prove the format yourself.

---

## 75. Master the Complete Process

Before outsourcing, learn how to perform:

* Niche research.
* Competitor analysis.
* Idea selection.
* Clip sourcing.
* Scriptwriting.
* Voice direction.
* Editing.
* Captioning.
* Publishing.
* Analytics review.

You should understand the process well enough to explain it simply and clearly.

A process is properly understood when it can be taught to someone else without confusion.

---

## 76. Outsourcing Milestone

Begin considering a team when:

* The first channel is monetized.
* You have personally created successful videos.
* You understand why they worked.
* You can reproduce the performance.
* The channel is making approximately $1,000 per month or more.
* The production process can be documented.

Do not start multiple channels before reaching this stage.

---

## 77. Outsource the Work You Least Enjoy

For many creators, editing will be the first activity delegated.

Possible retained responsibilities include:

* Niche research.
* Topic approval.
* Script approval.
* Quality control.
* Performance analysis.
* Team management.

The editor should execute an established standard.

---

## 78. Train Editors Thoroughly

Editor training should include:

* Live calls.
* Daily or regular communication.
* Reference videos.
* Good and bad examples.
* Step-by-step instructions.
* Clear quality standards.
* Explanations of why each element matters.
* Answers to recurring questions.

Treat editors respectfully and make it easy for them to ask questions.

The documentation should gradually reduce the number of questions required.

---

## 79. Create Standard Operating Procedures

The editor should receive documented instructions covering:

* How to choose the hook clip.
* How long clips should remain on screen.
* How to match clips to narration.
* How to use zooms.
* How to use arrows and circles.
* How loud music should be.
* Where sound effects should appear.
* How captions should be divided.
* What export settings to use.
* How to perform quality control.

The goal is to make video production consistent even when the channel owner is not editing.

---

# PART XXII: SCALING

## 80. Avoid Shiny-Object Syndrome

Do not immediately start several new channels after the first signs of success.

Dividing attention too early can damage the main channel.

Follow this order:

1. Build one channel.
2. Produce viral videos.
3. Reach monetization.
4. Generate consistent income.
5. Document the process.
6. Hire an editor.
7. Train the editor.
8. Confirm that quality remains stable.
9. Maintain the channel.
10. Begin the next channel.

---

## 81. Build Channels as Digital Assets

The scaling model is:

> Build → monetize → systemize → outsource → maintain → repeat.

Each successful channel should operate as a separate digital asset.

The owner’s role gradually shifts from editing to:

* Strategy.
* Research.
* Quality control.
* Hiring.
* Team management.
* New channel development.

The objective is to build multiple stable channels without weakening the original one.

---

# PART XXIII: VIDEO-EDITING AGENT CONVENTIONS

## 82. Mandatory Agent Inputs

Before editing, the agent should receive or identify:

* Target niche.
* Reference competitor.
* Reference outlier video.
* Winning format.
* Topic.
* Script.
* Voice-over.
* Source clips.
* Reveal clip.
* Music.
* Required aspect ratio.
* Target duration.

If any item is missing, the agent should derive it from the approved reference material where possible.

---

## 83. Mandatory Agent Analysis

Before assembling the video, the agent must determine:

* The strongest hook clip.
* The spoken phrase matched to each visual.
* The reveal position.
* The ideal duration.
* The clip-change frequency.
* The music beat-drop position.
* Where overlays are required.
* Where sound effects are required.
* Caption phrase divisions.

The agent must not begin by placing clips randomly on the timeline.

---

## 84. Hook Rules for Editing Agents

The first second must:

* Contain the strongest visual.
* Include movement.
* Clearly show the main subject.
* Create confusion or curiosity.
* Be understandable without audio.
* Avoid titles or introductions.

The hook clip may come from any point in the source footage.

Chronological order is secondary to attention.

---

## 85. Pacing Rules for Editing Agents

The agent must:

* Remove silence.
* Remove unnecessary pauses.
* Change or reframe visuals frequently.
* Match every visual to the narration.
* Maintain movement.
* Avoid repetitive footage.
* Reach the reveal quickly.
* End immediately after the payoff.

The agent should prefer compact storytelling over decorative editing.

---

## 86. Visual-Effect Rules for Editing Agents

The agent may use:

* Slow zooms.
* Pop-in circles.
* Arrows.
* Subject tracking.
* Subtle camera shake.
* Adjustment layers.
* Light saturation and contrast enhancement.
* Moderate sharpness.

The agent must not:

* Use excessive camera shake.
* Block the subject with overlays.
* Add effects without a clear purpose.
* Apply identical effects when clip framing requires adjustment.
* Overgrade the footage.

Every effect must improve attention, clarity or impact.

---

## 87. Audio Rules for Editing Agents

The agent must maintain this hierarchy:

1. Voice-over.
2. Music.
3. Sound effects.

The agent must:

* Keep narration clear.
* Reduce music beneath speech.
* Raise music near the reveal where appropriate.
* Align the beat drop with the payoff.
* Keep sound effects quiet.
* Match sound effects to visual actions.

The agent must reject audio that is jarring or distracting.

---

## 88. Caption Rules for Editing Agents

Captions must:

* Match the voice-over exactly.
* Be manually checked.
* Be divided into short phrases.
* Follow natural speech rhythm.
* Avoid overlapping.
* Avoid covering key actions.
* Use a readable bold font.
* Include a stroke and drop shadow.
* Remain visually consistent.

Caption timing must be corrected even when generated automatically.

---

## 89. Final Quality-Control Checklist

Before export, the editing agent must confirm:

### Hook

* Is the strongest visual first?
* Does the opening work on mute?
* Is the action immediately understandable?
* Is there a strong curiosity gap?

### Script and voice

* Is every line necessary?
* Is pronunciation correct?
* Is the delivery energetic?
* Are all gaps removed?
* Does the narration match the footage?

### Visuals

* Does every clip support the spoken phrase?
* Is there enough movement?
* Are clips changing frequently enough?
* Is the reveal clear?
* Is the video compact?

### Effects

* Are zooms smooth?
* Do arrows and circles direct attention?
* Is camera shake subtle?
* Is colour enhancement natural?

### Audio

* Is the voice the clearest element?
* Is music quiet enough?
* Does the beat drop support the payoff?
* Are sound effects subtle?

### Captions

* Are captions accurate?
* Are they correctly timed?
* Are phrases short?
* Do they avoid overlapping?
* Are they easy to read?

### Ending

* Does the ending fulfil the hook?
* Does the video stop immediately after the payoff?
* Is there any unnecessary trailing footage?

---

# PART XXIV: PERFORMANCE-IMPROVEMENT LOOP

## 90. Continuous Improvement Process

After every upload:

1. Record the topic.
2. Record the hook.
3. Record the duration.
4. Record the viewed-versus-swiped result.
5. Record average percentage viewed.
6. Compare the video with previous uploads.
7. Identify the strongest and weakest element.
8. Change the next video accordingly.

Do not continue producing the exact same weak format without adjustment.

---

## 91. Decision Rules

### When viewers swipe immediately

Change:

* The opening clip.
* The topic.
* The first spoken words.
* The first-frame composition.
* The level of movement.
* The curiosity gap.

### When viewers begin watching but leave early

Change:

* The pacing.
* Clip duration.
* Script length.
* Number of visual changes.
* Reveal timing.
* Strength of the payoff.

### When one video performs much better

Repeat:

* The topic category.
* The hook mechanism.
* The editing structure.
* The length.
* The reveal style.
* The emotional effect.

### When the format stops working

Research:

* New competitor outliers.
* Recent trends.
* New sub-niches.
* New visual mechanisms.
* Adjacent topics.

Then restart the testing cycle.

# FINAL OPERATING PRINCIPLE

The complete system is:

> Research proven demand, isolate the winning mechanism, create a new variation, build a strong visual hook, maintain fast pacing, deliver a clear payoff, publish consistently, study the statistics, double down on successful videos and scale only after mastering the process personally.

The tools may change.

The niche may change.

The trends may change.

The operating loop remains:

> Research → Produce → Publish → Measure → Improve → Double down → Systemize → Outsource → Scale.
